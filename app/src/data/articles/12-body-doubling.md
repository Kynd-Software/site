# Body Doubling: Why Working Alongside Others Helps

It sounds almost too simple to be real. Sit next to someone — or even have someone present on a video call — and suddenly the task you've been avoiding for days becomes doable. This phenomenon is called body doubling, and for many people with ADHD, it's one of the most effective strategies available.

## What body doubling is

Body doubling is the practice of having another person present while you work on a task. The other person doesn't need to help with the task, comment on it, or even be aware of what you're doing. Their mere presence is what makes the difference.

It might look like:

- Working in a café instead of at home
- Sitting in the same room as a partner while doing admin
- Joining a virtual co-working session where everyone works silently on their own tasks
- Having a friend on a phone call while you clean the kitchen
- Working in a library rather than alone at your desk

The other person is an anchor — a gentle external presence that helps the ADHD brain stay on track.

## Why it works

The exact mechanisms aren't fully established in research, but several theories align with what we know about ADHD neurology:

### External accountability

Even mild, implicit accountability helps. When someone is present, there's a subtle social pressure to stay on task. The brain registers "someone can see me" as a low-level form of external structure — enough to generate the small dopamine boost needed to maintain engagement.

This isn't about judgement. It's about the social brain providing what the executive function brain struggles to generate internally.

### Arousal regulation

Working alone on a low-stimulation task means the ADHD brain has to generate all its own arousal — its own reason to stay alert and engaged. Another person's presence adds just enough sensory and social input to raise the arousal baseline without being distracting.

It's the difference between trying to read in a silent, empty room (understimulating) and reading in a café with background hum (just enough external input to keep the brain online).

### Reduced mind-wandering

The default mode network (the brain network active during mind-wandering) tends to intrude more in ADHD. The presence of another person may help suppress this network — the social awareness keeps the brain slightly more externally oriented, reducing the tendency to drift into internal thought.

### Mirroring and entrainment

Humans naturally synchronise with those around them. Sitting near someone who is calmly focused can entrain your own nervous system toward calm focus. This isn't conscious — it's a basic social neuroscience phenomenon.

### Making the task "real"

For ADHD brains, tasks that feel abstract or unwitnessed can struggle to feel real enough to engage with. Having someone present makes the task more concrete — it exists in social reality, not just in your mental to-do list.

## What the research says

While body doubling specifically hasn't been extensively studied under that name, related phenomena are well-documented:

- **Social facilitation theory** (Zajonc, 1965) shows that the presence of others enhances performance on simple or well-practised tasks
- **Accountability research** consistently shows that external accountability improves follow-through
- **Co-regulation research** demonstrates that proximity to calm others helps regulate the nervous system
- **Environmental design research** in ADHD supports the principle that external structure compensates for internal executive function difficulties

The ADHD coaching community has used body doubling for decades, with consistent anecdotal reports of effectiveness. Formal research is beginning to catch up.

## How to use it

### In person
- Work in cafés, libraries, or co-working spaces
- Ask a friend or partner to simply be in the room
- Join study groups (even if studying different things)
- Parallel play — both people doing their own tasks, together

### Virtually
- Virtual co-working sessions (many are ADHD-specific)
- Video calls where both people work silently with cameras on
- Focusmate, Flow Club, or similar platforms designed for this
- Discord servers with "study with me" voice channels

### Ambient presence
- "Study with me" videos or livestreams
- Working with a podcast or audiobook (for some, this provides enough "presence")
- Having a pet nearby (genuinely — the presence of another living being can help)

## When it doesn't work

Body doubling isn't universal. Some situations where it might not help:

- If the other person is a source of distraction or social anxiety
- If the task requires deep creative thought that social presence disrupts
- If you're in a hyperfocus state (you don't need the external anchor — you're locked in)
- If the relationship dynamic makes you feel judged rather than supported

The key is that the presence should feel neutral to positive — supportive without being scrutinising.

## Why it's not a crutch

Some people worry that relying on body doubling means they "should" be able to do it alone. This reflects the internalised belief that ADHD difficulties are willpower failures.

Using environmental support for a neurological difference isn't a crutch any more than wearing glasses is a crutch for short-sightedness. It's using available resources to compensate for a genuine difference in how your brain functions.

If body doubling helps you get things done with less suffering, use it freely and without apology.
